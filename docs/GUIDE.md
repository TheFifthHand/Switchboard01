# Two short guides

Start SWITCHBOARD first (Windows: double-click **Start SWITCHBOARD.bat** in the release zip; see
`START HERE.txt`). It opens in your browser at `http://127.0.0.1:4173/`.

## Make your first loop (about two minutes)

1. Press **Jump In**. A House groove starts, and the lit pads in the Loops grid say **Playing**.
2. **Swap a part.** Tap another pad in the **Bass** column, for example *Rolling*. It shows
   **Next bar** and joins on the next bar line, in time. Only one clip per part plays at a time.
3. **Change the sound.** Tap a part's name at the top of its column to select it. In the panel on
   the right, turn **Tone** (darker ↔ brighter) and **Space** (room around the sound): drag a knob up
   or down, or focus it and use the arrow keys. **Change** next to the sound name opens the sound
   browser with other kits and presets.
4. **Play along.** Use the little keyboard at the bottom, or your computer keys (A S D F … for white
   keys, W E T Y … for black keys). **Musical Assist** is on, so melodic parts stay in the song's key.
5. **Make it yours.** Press **Variation** in the part panel for a new take on that part's pattern.
   Don't like it? **Undo** (Ctrl+Z) brings the old one back. **Lock** keeps a part exactly as it is.
6. **Draw a beat.** Switch the pad area to **Steps**, choose a drum sound, and click steps on or off.
7. **Peek at the cables.** Click the **Cables** bar under the pads (*Show the patch*). Each part runs
   Instrument → Drive → Filter → Channel → Master. Unplug a cable and the part drops out;
   **Restore Connection** (or Ctrl+Z) puts it back. The **Shape** view shows every setting, the six
   macros and the effects rack.

Your work saves automatically in this browser; the transport shows **Saved**.

## Record and export (about two minutes)

**Record a performance**

1. With music playing, press **Performance** in the RECORD group of the transport. It turns coral
   and the caption reads *Recording performance*.
2. Play: tap pads, launch whole rows with the scene buttons on the right (Intro, Groove, Lift,
   Break), play keys, turn knobs. Cables and sound choices are locked while you record, so the take
   replays exactly as you played it.
3. Press **Performance** again to stop. The take (*Take 1*) appears in **Arrange → Performances**,
   where you can replay it, rename it, end it earlier, change recorded values or delete actions.

**Record notes into a clip** (optional)

Select a part and a clip, press **Notes**, and play the keyboard or drum pads. The caption shows the
timing grid it snaps to. Grid, metronome and count-in are in the recording options (the metronome
button next to **Performance**). Press **Notes** again to stop; one Undo removes the whole pass.

**Export a WAV**

1. Press **Export** (the dark download button at the top right; on narrow windows it is in the
   **⋯** menu).
2. In **What to export**, choose the clips playing now, a scene, the **Song (arrangement)** or a
   **Performance**.
3. Set **Tail** (seconds for echoes to ring out), **Sample rate**, **Bit depth** and the file name,
   then press **Export WAV**. A progress bar shows the render; **Cancel export** stops it. The file is
   a stereo PCM WAV rendered by the same engine you hear, not a recording of your speakers.

**Keep a portable copy**

Open the projects button (folder icon, top right) → **Export this project**. The `.sb01.zip` file
holds all musical state and your imported recordings. **Import project file…** opens it again in any
browser. Browser storage is working storage; project files are your backup.
